# The rule RFCs renumbered and restated in the owner's order

- **Date:** 2026-10-24
- **Type:** process
- **Scope:** `design`, `site`, `skills`, `docs`
- **PR:** [#298](https://github.com/Prism-Shadow/model-message-stream-protocol/pull/298)

[中文版](2026-10-24-rfc-renumbering.zh.md)

## What changed

- The rule RFCs took MMSP-RFC-0002 to MMSP-RFC-0013 in the order of the owner's twelve titles, which became their Chinese H1s. Their English titles are, in order: One protocol for every model; Every request is an async stream; Messages stay lean; Essential fields are never dropped; Client routing is explicit; Keys stay safe; History does not change; Every error is typed; Every thinking level is supported; Request parameters are never fuzzy-matched; A request can be interrupted; Implementation code is straightforward. [MMSP-RFC-0001](../../design/rfcs/MMSP-RFC-0001-rfc-process.md) stayed the RFC process and gained a table from the numbers of 0.5.5 to the new ones.
- The content of the rules of 0.5.5 moved to the RFC of its topic: the stream grammar stayed MMSP-RFC-0003; routing went to MMSP-RFC-0006, and the explicit version inside a client to MMSP-RFC-0011; the credential scope to MMSP-RFC-0007; thinking levels to MMSP-RFC-0010; typed rejection to MMSP-RFC-0009; minimal replay to MMSP-RFC-0004, with `tool_call_id` and unsigned thinking in MMSP-RFC-0005; two packages, one contract into MMSP-RFC-0002; stop metadata, `IncompleteStreamError` and the thinking round trip into MMSP-RFC-0005; direct conversion to MMSP-RFC-0013, with its "nothing redundant" in MMSP-RFC-0004. The files of the old MMSP-RFC-0009 and MMSP-RFC-0011 were removed.
- Four rules were written from the code of 0.5.6: MMSP-RFC-0008 from the history copies of [#291](https://github.com/Prism-Shadow/model-message-stream-protocol/pull/291); MMSP-RFC-0009, widened from a rejected value to every error, from [#288](https://github.com/Prism-Shadow/model-message-stream-protocol/pull/288) and [#290](https://github.com/Prism-Shadow/model-message-stream-protocol/pull/290); MMSP-RFC-0011, whose table is the configuration page of [#289](https://github.com/Prism-Shadow/model-message-stream-protocol/pull/289); MMSP-RFC-0012 from the interruption of [#292](https://github.com/Prism-Shadow/model-message-stream-protocol/pull/292).
- Every rule was restated from the code at 0.5.6, with these statements corrected to it: embeddings come from an endpoint that does not stream; `google-genai` puts the placeholder thought signature on a turn's first call when nothing signed it; `ant-messages` forces a tool as `{type: "tool", name}`; Claude Fable 5 runs `none` at the lowest effort; Kimi K3 takes `none` as it is ([#294](https://github.com/Prism-Shadow/model-message-stream-protocol/pull/294)); a gateway's encrypted reasoning block is recorded as `encrypted_content`, `format` and `reasoning_id` ([#287](https://github.com/Prism-Shadow/model-message-stream-protocol/pull/287)); `ApiKeyRequiredError` is also raised for a client given no key at all ([#288](https://github.com/Prism-Shadow/model-message-stream-protocol/pull/288)); the abort error is the one built-in error MMSP raises outside `integration/`; the Python `mmsp` client, aborted while its caller is busy between reads, closes its request at the caller's next read; the in-item shapes of earlier releases read until 0.6.0 are a thought signature as bytes, a reasoning item without its channel and the 0.5.5 gateway reasoning block, while a tool call id equal to the function name is still recorded by `google-genai`. The `Enforced by` sections name files, not private classes.
- `design/README.md` and `design/README.zh.md` indexed the new numbers and recorded the renumbering. `AGENTS.md` and the `mmsp-dev` skill cited the new numbers by content and gained the rules on history (MMSP-RFC-0008) and interruption (MMSP-RFC-0012). The RFC links of the 0.5.5 entries were pointed at the RFC that holds their content, with "now MMSP-RFC-NNNN" where the number changed. `site/astro.config.mjs` redirected each old RFC URL, under `/docs/` and `/rfcs/` in both languages, to the RFC that holds its content.
